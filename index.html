Department leaderboard

Eco points = kg × action weight (reuse 12, refurbish 10, repair 10, hazard hold 8, recycle 6), plus 20% once the item reaches Recovered. Reuse scores higher because it prevents waste instead of treating it.

Do

  • Back up and factory-reset phones, laptops and PCs before handing them over.
  • Hand e-waste only to authorised collectors or recyclers.
  • Keep batteries inside the device unless they come out safely.
  • Donate or swap working devices before they become waste.

Don't

  • Don't burn cables or boards to get at the copper. It releases toxic fumes.
  • Don't open monitors, tubes or batteries yourself.
  • Don't put e-waste in the normal dry-waste bin.
  • Don't keep swollen batteries charging or in a bag.

The rules

India's E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 use Extended Producer Responsibility: producers, manufacturers and importers must make sure the electronics they sell are collected and recycled by authorised recyclers. A system like this makes that chain easy for the user and visible to auditors.

Storage

Where the data is stored

What is saved

  • manifests: ID, device, quantity, kg, action, stage, drop point, department, pickup date and slot, time created.
  • swaps: swap-board listings and whether they are claimed.
  • No names, emails or phone numbers are stored.

Who can see and change it

  • Anyone who can open this page can read the records.
  • Contributors and Editors of the page can add and change records. View-only people can read only.
  • Inside the page, a customer sees only their own manifests. An admin (demo PIN) sees all, moves items through the stages and edits hours. The PIN is a demo, not real security.
  • The data stays when the page is updated, and is erased if the page is deleted.

If the shared database is not available

  • The page falls back to this browser's own storage (localStorage), visible only to you on this device.
  • The top of the page always tells you which one is in use.

How the system works

Input

  • Device type, quantity, age, condition
  • Battery state, optional repair quote and new price
  • Area, department, handover choice, pickup date and slot
  • Drop point list with coordinates and opening hours
  • Typical material composition table per device
→

Processing

  • Rule engine picks Reuse, Repair, Refurbish, Hazard hold or Recycle
  • Weight × composition gives recoverable material and CO₂e estimate
  • Haversine distance ranks capable drop points; hours give open-now status
  • Slot capacity check, manifest stored, eco points computed
→

Output

  • Action, reason and safe-handling checklist
  • Map, hours, directions link, pickup booking
  • Tracking timeline, receipt, CSV export, swap listing
  • Campus dashboard and department leaderboard

Expected environmental benefits

  • Fewer devices dumped in landfills or burned in the open, so lead, mercury, cadmium and brominated flame retardants stay out of soil and water.
  • Working and repairable devices stay in use longer, which avoids the energy and mining needed to make new ones.
  • Copper, aluminium, silver, gold and cobalt return to the supply chain as secondary raw material.
  • Swollen or leaking batteries are flagged early, which lowers fire and toxic-leak risk.
  • Tracking IDs and stage status make informal scrap handling visible and support audits under e-waste rules.

Future scope

  • Photo upload with an image classifier that identifies the device type and visible damage.
  • QR labels on each item, scanned at every stage.
  • IoT bins that report fill level so pickups are scheduled automatically.
  • Live feed of the authorised recycler list from the pollution control authorities.

Limits of this prototype

Drop points, hours and drives are made-up sample entries. Material and CO₂e figures are rough per-unit estimates for typical devices, meant for comparison only. A full version would use the official recycler list and measured composition data from recyclers.
